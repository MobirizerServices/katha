## 0. Read this first — a contradiction to resolve

The PDD states the launch catalogue two ways that cannot both be true:

- **§7.3** — "25–30 series live at soft launch: **≥ 10 Hindi originals, ≥ 5 each Tamil and Telugu**, plus dubs" → that is **≥ 20 originals**, i.e. 67–80% originals.
- **§23.2** — launch **originals : licensed dubs = 40 : 60** → that is **~12 originals** of 30.

At Tier B economics (₹15–25 lakh per 60-episode original), the difference is roughly **₹12–20 crore of launch content spend** — not a rounding error, and it compounds the content-spend triplet already open in the review log.

**This bible builds to the §23.2 reading** (14 originals : 16 acquired) because it is the affordable one, and because §31.1's ₹2.5 Cr/month cannot fund the §7.3 reading. **Recommended fix:** restate §7.3 as *"≥ 12 Hindi titles, ≥ 6 each Tamil and Telugu, of which ≥ 14 are Katha originals"* — same shelf-facing promise, honest economics. Take it to the same decision session as the LTV/CAC and content-spend items.

## 1. What Katha content is (the one-paragraph brief every writer gets)

> A Katha series is a **60–90 second** vertical episode, 45–70 episodes long, in Hindi, Tamil or Telugu, set in a **specific Indian place you could point to on a map**. Every episode opens on motion or a line already in progress — never an establishing shot — and ends on a reversal, a reveal or a threat. The premise is legible by **0:30 of episode 1**. Episodes 1–10 are free and must be good enough that a stranger watches all ten; **episode 10 ends on the hardest turn in the series** and episode 11 pays it off within 20 seconds. Nobody in a Katha series is humiliated for their caste, religion, region or body. Every title passes the **"watch it with your family on a train"** test.

### 1.1 The five pillars, made operational (PDD §23.1)

| Pillar | What it means on the page | How we check it |
|---|---|---|
| Vernacular with local texture | Named neighbourhood, real food, real transport, correct kinship terms and honorifics | A reader from that city reads E1–E5 and can't find a false note |
| Emotional velocity | A reversal, reveal or threat every 60–90 s — i.e. **one per episode, minimum** | Beat-sheet audit: every episode row carries a hook type from §3.2 |
| Star-adjacent casting | One recognisable TV/OTT face per Tier A; rising creators elsewhere | Casting brief attached at greenlight, not after |
| Quality bar above free rivals | Lit faces, clean dialogue at −16 LUFS, real locations, honest subtitles | §7.2 auto-QC gate + human QC pass |
| Respectful content | No caste/religion/gender shock value; no real-person defamation | Legal read at outline stage; the train test at script lock |

### 1.2 Hard content rules (rejection criteria — a script that breaks one comes back)

1. No caste slurs, caste-coded humiliation, or "reveal" plots whose turn is a character's caste.
2. No religious ridicule, no religious violence as spectacle, no communal framing of a villain.
3. No sexual violence on screen. Coercion may be a story fact; it is never a shot. No minors in any romantic or sexualised frame, ever.
4. Domestic violence may be depicted only where the story holds it to account within three episodes.
5. Suicide and self-harm: never method-explicit; helpline card mandatory on the episode (§15).
6. No real, identifiable person, brand, logo, place of worship interior, or police/armed-forces insignia without written clearance.
7. Substance use is not glamorised; no smoking without the statutory disclaimer.
8. Nothing that would fail a U/A 16+ self-classification. **Katha ships no "A" content in v1** — it narrows the store rating, the family test, and the ad inventory we can buy.

## 3. The story machine

Everything below is reusable. A room that internalises §3 can produce a competent Katha outline in a day; the LangGraph writers'-room assist (§22.2) is prompted from these same tables.

### 3.1 Episode shape (60–90 s)

| Segment | Time | Job |
|---|---|---|
| **Cold open** | 0:00–0:05 | Mid-motion or mid-line. No establishing shot, no logo, no "previously on" before E5. |
| **Orient** | 0:05–0:15 | One line or one image that tells a returning viewer where we are. |
| **Escalate** | 0:15–0:50 | One want, one obstacle, one attempt. Exactly one scene; two locations maximum. |
| **Turn** | 0:50–1:10 | The reversal / reveal / threat. This is the episode's reason to exist. |
| **Cut** | last 3–5 s | Hard cut on the turn. Never resolve, never a reaction montage, never a soft fade. |

Rules of thumb: **≤ 55 words of dialogue** per episode · **≥ 1 close-up on whoever loses the beat** · title card only on E1 · the "to be continued" plate only where the turn needs a breath.

### 3.2 The twelve hooks (every episode ends on one — label it in the beat sheet)

| # | Hook | Shape | Ration |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Arrival** | Someone who should not be here walks in | Anywhere |
| 2 | **Overheard** | A character hears the truth they were being protected from | Anywhere |
| 3 | **Object** | A photo, letter, key or ledger contradicts the story we were told | Anywhere |
| 4 | **Confession** | Someone says the thing out loud, to the wrong person | Max 1 per 10 eps |
| 5 | **Reversal of power** | The one begging becomes the one deciding | High value — save for cliffs |
| 6 | **False ally** | The helper turns out to work for the antagonist | Max 2 per series |
| 7 | **Deadline** | A clock starts (nine days, one season, until the wedding) | Strong at E1 and E11 |
| 8 | **Body** | Someone is hurt, taken, or missing | Never gratuitous |
| 9 | **Public shame** | The private fact becomes public in front of the family | Family-drama staple |
| 10 | **Identity crack** | The audience learns who someone really is before the hero does | The dramatic-irony engine |
| 11 | **Choice** | Two doors, both costly; the episode ends before the choice | Max 1 per 12 eps |
| 12 | **Return** | The dead, gone or exiled comes back | Once per series |

**Anti-patterns:** the dream reveal; a misunderstanding one sentence would fix (unless the sentence is *prevented* on screen); a hook that only works if someone behaves stupidly; two consecutive episodes ending on the same hook type.

### 3.3 The free-to-paid cliff (E10 → E11) — the most important 30 seconds in the series

The cliff is engineered backwards from the paywall, not discovered in the edit.

**E10 must:**
1. Land on hook **5 (reversal of power)**, **10 (identity crack)** or **12 (return)** — the three that convert. Never a Deadline; viewers assume a clock resolves itself.
2. **Answer one question carried since E1 and open a bigger one in the same shot.** Payoff *then* debt — a pure cliff with no payoff reads as a con and shows up as refunds.
3. Keep the **protagonist** in frame at the cut, not a side character.
4. Run **5–8 seconds longer** than a normal episode. The turn needs air, and the paused frame under the paywall sheet (§8.4) is the sales asset.
5. Ship a deliberately chosen **cliffhanger frame** as a still (§7.1) — the paywall hero image and the clip-factory thumbnail.

**E11 must** answer the E10 turn within **20 seconds**. The single largest driver of refunds and one-star reviews in this format is an E11 that stalls. It then ends on its own hook, of a different type.

**Scorecard (§23.6):** cliff conversion ≥ 8% within 7 days. Below 2% after 7 days → stop promotion and re-cut **E1 first, then E10** — a weak cliff is usually a weak premise arriving late.

### 3.4 Series architecture (60-episode standard)

| Block | Eps | Job | Ends on |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Hook** | 1–3 | Premise, protagonist's want, the world's rule | The rule turning against her |
| **Build** | 4–9 | The antagonist gets a face; an ally arrives; the first real cost | E9: the ally wavers |
| **Cliff** | 10 | Payoff + bigger debt (§3.3) | Reversal / identity crack / return |
| **Pay-off run** | 11–20 | Answer fast (E11), then she goes on offence | E20: first defeat |
| **Deepen** | 21–35 | The antagonist's motive is humanised; a Hook-block secret returns | E35: false ally revealed |
| **Squeeze** | 36–48 | Everything costs; she loses the ally, the place, or the name | E48: lowest point |
| **Turn** | 49–56 | She wins using the thing established in the Hook block | E56: the mask comes off |
| **Land** | 57–60 | Resolution plus one door left open | A held look, not a wedding |

**Secondary paywalls:** unlock rates dip at E20 and E36. Programme a stronger hook at **E19** and **E35** to carry viewers across.

### 3.5 Series-bible template (the Concept-gate one-pager)

```
TITLE (script + transliteration + literal meaning)
LANGUAGE / SETTING (city, neighbourhood, season, year)
LOGLINE (<= 35 words; contains the want and the obstacle)
TIER / EPISODES / DROP MODE
PROTAGONIST (name, age, job, the want, the wound, what she would never do)
ANTAGONIST (name, the want that collides, why he thinks he is right)
THE RULE (the one social/family/economic law the story tests)
CAST OF SIX (no more; one per episode block)
THE CLIFF (the E10 turn in one sentence + hook type)
SEASON SPINE (8 rows = the 3.4 blocks)
COMPARABLES (2 titles + their scorecard numbers)
COMPLIANCE FLAGS (rating, descriptors, clearance risks)
```

**Localisation standard for every acquired title:** names, places, food, festivals and kinship terms adapted to the target market; on-screen text **replaced, not subtitled over**; labelled **"Dubbed"** on the series page. ## 7. Operating cadence

| Ritual | When | Output |
|---|---|---|
| Concept gate | Rolling | §3.5 one-pager scored on the §23.4 rubric; kill or advance |
| Writers' room | 2 days per title | Season spine (§3.4) + E1–E12 beat sheet with hook labels |
| Outline gate | Before script | Legal read (defamation, likeness, clearance) + the train test |
| Script lock | −3 weeks from shoot | ≤ 55 words of dialogue per episode; safe zones marked in the script |
| Pilot test | On the E1–E5 cut | 200–500 beta users; go/no-go at E1→E3 ≥ 60%, intent ≥ 70% |
| Programming meeting | Weekly | Drop calendar four weeks out; bench ≥ 6 finished series per language |
| Scorecard review | Day 14 and Day 90 | §23.6 metrics → renew, sequel, or shelve |

**Bench rule:** never launch a language you cannot feed for six weeks. Better to launch Telugu in week 20 with a bench than in week 16 without one.
