Katha — iOS app design, v0.3

Every screen from the PDD screen inventory (§10.5), plus the alternate states each one needs, drawn on the §10.1 tokens. Use the state buttons under a frame to switch states. Series, characters and people are fictional. Thumbnails are keyword-matched Creative-Commons photos (LoremFlickr, e.g. indian,bride for a family saga, businessman,office for the office romance), locked so each series keeps the same photo, tinted to its palette, with the gradient as offline fallback. They stand in for studio key art; swap any by editing its img src or upload your own posters and they can be wired in directly.

1. Onboarding and login

No login is needed to watch free episodes. Language and interests take under 20 seconds; the app lands on Home as a guest. Login appears only at value moments — first unlock, first purchase, saving a series.

2. Discover

Home leads with one large For You card (muted trailer) and rows beneath. Browse and Search are for people who know what they want. The series page does one job: get the viewer into episode 1 or back to where they left off.

3. Watch and pay

The player hides its chrome after 2.5 s. Swipe up for the next episode, tap to pause, double-tap to like, long-press for 2×. The paywall is a sheet over the paused frame — the story stays visible, which is the strongest reason to unlock.

4. Account

Profile is the hub for money, saved series and settings. Guests see the same screen with one card explaining what an account protects. Settings keep language, data, notifications and the parental lock one level deep.

5. States and feedback

Nothing is ever a blank screen. Toasts confirm what changed in the same words as the button that caused it; errors say what happened and what to do; empty states point somewhere.

Notes

Photo placeholders: every poster and video frame carries a keyword-matched Creative-Commons photo (for example loremflickr.com/240/360/indian,bride?lock=11) tinted with the series gradient at 42% multiply so the board stays cohesive. Because these are CC-licensed Flickr photos, they are for internal review only — do not publish the board with them, and attribute or replace before any external use. Production key art from studios follows the cover-art guidelines in PDD §25.2 (face-forward, high contrast, title-safe zones) and replaces these one-for-one. Cast and avatars stay as initials on purpose — no real faces stand in for fictional characters.
